How I solve problems

Three problems from three different machines. Each one: what was happening, what was broken, what I looked at, what I decided, what I did, and what changed.

Case 01Tesla · Cybertruck · production

A production-critical half-shaft installation issue

Context

Cybertruck chassis launch. The half-shaft installation was on the critical path to production.

Problem

Vehicles made abnormal noise while moving. A teardown showed the half-shaft splines wearing into each other.

Investigation

Traced to a C-clip that was not fully seated during installation. If the clip's gap was not oriented correctly, there was a 20–30% chance it would not catch.

Decision

Contain on the line now and fix the design in parallel, instead of waiting for a redesign to unblock builds.

Execution

Added an operator check to rotate the clip to the correct orientation, validated it on 10 vehicles in an end-of-shift trial, and rolled it out line-wide. An engineer and the supplier worked the permanent design change. I worked on and off hours and kept executives updated.

Result

A production-critical issue contained across the line and handed to engineering and the supplier for the permanent fix.

  • Root-cause analysis
  • Containment
  • Line validation
  • Supplier + engineering
  • Exec communication
Case 02Rivian · R1T / R1S · suspension hydraulics

Suspension hydraulic fittings with swelling O-rings

Context

R1T and R1S suspension hydraulic lines. O-rings sit in the damper fittings where the lines connect.

Problem

O-rings were swelling, which made installation difficult and risked leaks or bursts during fluid fill. The failure mode could mean fluid loss, suspension problems, and limp mode.

Investigation

Tested the supplier process, transportation, and O-ring and fluid compatibility. Fluid alone did not cause the swelling. The dust caps, the last part touching the O-ring before install, were collecting fluid, so I brought in a polymer expert from the failure-analysis lab.

Decision

Fix the cause, not only the symptom: change the cap material, and contain the line with replacement O-rings in the meantime.

Execution

Ran a test plan submerging O-rings and caps, used safety data sheets to test material incompatibility, got repeatable results, sourced a TPE cap, and implemented the change with the supplier and purchasing team. Operators could swap O-rings at the line and keep building.

Result

Root cause confirmed with repeatable tests, and TPE caps validated and rolled in with the supplier, with the line running throughout.

  • Root-cause analysis
  • Materials testing
  • Supplier management
  • Containment
  • Failure analysis
Case 03Zoox · L5 program · test infrastructure

Test-asset demand across a robotaxi program

Context

Zoox test assets, such as lighting rigs, sensor-contamination bots, and vehicle-level builds, serve many teams across integration, system, and component testing.

Problem

Requests came in ad hoc. Sister teams added requirements mid-build, which caused scope creep and put downstream timelines at risk.

Investigation

Compared requested against actual asset time by team, week by week, and found assets underused and teams overrunning their slots.

Decision

Replace case-by-case negotiation with one intake and a standing committee, so every request is challenged and every team has a voice.

Execution

Created a Jira intake and a one-pager (capability, timeline, milestone), a forum of prototyping, controller validation, EMC, optics, supply chain, and HW/SW integration, phased builds, and Gantt charts, weekly updates, and epics.

Result

3 system-level assets on the roadmap, a sensor-contamination bench for a new sensor generation in about 2 weeks against a typical 4 to 6, a Drive Module bench that cut supplier bring-up by 3 weeks, and 20% faster issue triage.

  • Process design
  • Requirements
  • Cross-functional forum
  • Jira + Smartsheet
  • Phased delivery
